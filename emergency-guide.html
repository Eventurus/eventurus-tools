Lost or Stolen Passport

This happens more often than you'd think. Here is the sequence to follow.

  1. Report to the police immediately. Go to the nearest police station (派出所, pàichūsuǒ). You need a Report of Loss (遗失证明, yíshī zhèngmíng) — this is your legal proof of identity while you sort out a replacement. Dial 110 and they will direct you to the correct station.
  2. Contact your embassy or consulate. Apply for an Emergency Travel Document (emergency passport). This typically takes 1–4 working days. Most embassies have 24-hour emergency phone lines for their citizens.
  3. Cancel affected cards. Call your bank to freeze cards. Most international banks have collect-call numbers in China.
  4. Check your travel insurance. Most policies cover passport replacement costs and provide a 24-hour assistance hotline. They can also arrange emergency cash transfers.
  5. Use the police report to rebook travel. Airlines and high-speed train operators accept the police report of loss as temporary ID. Keep it with you at all times.
💡 Pro tip: Keep a photocopy of your passport photo page and current visa sticker in your phone's photo album and in your luggage separately. It significantly speeds up the replacement process.

Pharmacies & Over-the-Counter Medications

Pharmacies (药店, yàodiàn) are everywhere in Chinese cities. Green cross sign, open 08:00–22:00 typically. Some are 24-hour.

💊 What you can buy without a prescription

  • ✓ Basic first aid: Band-aids, disinfectant (碘伏, diǎnfú), gauze, surgical masks — all widely available
  • ✓ Cold & flu: Paracetamol (对乙酰氨基酚, duìyǐxiān'ānjīfēn), ibuprofen (布洛芬, bùluòfēn), cough syrup
  • ✓ Digestive: Antacids, activated charcoal, oral rehydration salts (口服补液盐)
  • ✓ Allergies: Loratadine (氯雷他定, lǜléitādìng) — common OTC antihistamine
  • ✓ Travel health: Motion sickness tablets, anti-diarrhoeal (蒙脱石散, méngtuōshí sǎn)

🔴 What requires a prescription

  • ✗ Antibiotics — strictly prescription-only (buying without one is illegal)
  • ✗ Strong painkillers — codeine, tramadol, morphine are tightly controlled
  • ✗ Psychiatric medications — antidepressants, anxiolytics require a Chinese prescription
  • ✗ Pseudoephedrine products — most cold medications containing it are controlled
💊 Pharmacy tips: Show the pharmacist the Chinese name of the medication on your phone. Major chains like Walvax, Guoda, and Da Sheng Lin are reliable. In Beijing and Shanghai, some pharmacies near embassies stock imported medications — expect to pay 2–3× the Chinese equivalent price.

Travel Insurance & Medical Coverage

China does not provide free emergency healthcare to foreign visitors. Travel insurance is not optional — it is essential.

🛡️ Minimum coverage you need: Medical evacuation/repatriation (¥500,000+), emergency treatment, hospital admission, and lost baggage. Most international travel insurance policies like World Nomads, SafetyWing, and AXA include China. Check the fine print for adventure sports — hiking in Zhangjiajie or skiing in Harbin may require a rider.

📋 What to carry at all times

  • ✓ Insurance card — printed or saved offline on your phone
  • ✓ Passport photocopy (photo page + visa sticker)
  • ✓ Emergency contact numbers — insurance hotline, next of kin, your embassy
  • ✓ Blood type and known allergies — written in Chinese (ask a hotel concierge to write it)

Frequently Asked Questions

Common concerns travellers have about emergencies in China.

Is 120 (ambulance) free in China?
No. Ambulance services in China are not free. Expect to pay ¥100–300 for the call-out plus ¥5–10 per kilometre. Some international insurance policies reimburse ambulance costs — check your policy. In an emergency, do not hesitate to call 120 regardless of cost.
Can I use my foreign health insurance in China?
Most standard health insurance plans from your home country do not cover overseas treatment. You need dedicated travel insurance or an international health insurance plan. United Family Hospital, ParkwayHealth, and similar international clinics direct-bill many international insurers including Allianz, AXA, Cigna, and Bupa — call them to confirm before you go.
What happens if I need surgery in China?
Major hospitals in Beijing (Peking Union, PLA General), Shanghai (Ruijin, Huashan), and Guangzhou (Sun Yat-sen) have international-grade surgical departments. You will need to pay a deposit (¥50,000–200,000 for major surgery) upfront if you are not using an insurance direct-billing facility. International clinics can arrange medical evacuation to Hong Kong, Singapore, or your home country if needed.
What if I need prescription medication that I take regularly?
Bring enough for your entire trip plus a reserve (2–3 weeks extra). Carry the original packaging and a doctor's note (translated into Chinese) explaining the medication. Some medications available over the counter in other countries (codeine, certain antidepressants) are tightly controlled in China. Contact the nearest Chinese embassy before travelling if you are unsure about a specific medication.
Can I get a refund if I prepay at a hospital?
Yes. Public hospitals bill on a deposit system — you pay ¥3,000–10,000 upfront for emergency treatment, costs are deducted, and the remainder is refunded at discharge. Keep all receipts and medical records for your insurance claim. International clinics usually settle directly with your insurer so no deposit is needed.
What if I am in a city without an international hospital?
In smaller cities, go to the largest public hospital (usually called People's Hospital, 人民医院). Ask for the VIP / International Department (国际医疗部) — many Tier-2 city hospitals have one. They may not have dedicated English staff on site, but they can connect to a telephone interpreter service. Alternatively, get a taxi or Didi to the nearest city with an international hospital (see the hospital table above).
