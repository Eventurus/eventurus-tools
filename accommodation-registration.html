Accommodation Registration in China

Registering where you stay: for anyone not in a hotel it is now an online form, and in Shanghai there are two systems to choose between.

The short version

  • Who. Foreign visitors staying anywhere in China other than a hotel, including with friends, in a rented flat, in a dormitory or in a serviced apartment, must register their address with the local public security organ. Residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan register too.
  • Deadline. Registration must be completed within 24 hours of arriving at the address. Either the visitor or the host may file it, and the first registration at an address is made with the host's help.
  • How. Nationwide since 21 September 2026 it can be done online, through the National Immigration Administration platform at s.nia.gov.cn or the NIA 12367 app and mini programs. In Shanghai the city's own system at gaj.sh.gov.cn/crj/24hr is also open, and it is the one residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan are directed to use.
  • Hotels. Hotel guests do not register themselves. The hotel registers every foreign guest, reports the record to the police and prints the registration form at check-in.
  • Proof. Online registration leaves a record that is accepted in place of the paper form. The printed Registration Form of Residence for Visitors from Overseas is available at any public security window nearby.
Verified 24 September 2026 against the National Immigration Administration's Announcement No. 7 of 2026 and the Shanghai Exit and Entry Administration Division's guide of 21 September 2026. Online registration carries the same legal effect as registering at a police station, and the offline channel remains open. Seven regions (Hebei, Liaoning, Zhejiang, Hubei, Guangxi, Chongqing and Sichuan) had piloted the service from 20 March 2026.

What the law asks for

Article 39 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law splits the duty in two. In a hotel, the hotel registers you under the public security rules for the hotel industry and reports the record to the local public security organ. In any other place, you or your host must register with the public security organ for that address within 24 hours after check-in.

Since 21 September 2026 the second half of that duty can be discharged online anywhere in China. The obligation itself has not changed, only the way you meet it. Registering online and registering in person are legally equivalent, and going to the counter is still an option.

Who needs to register

If you stay in a hotel

Nothing for you to file. The hotel handles the registration and reports it, and it prints the accommodation registration form for you. In Shanghai, any hotel can complete the procedure. You need only present a valid identification document.

One thing registration does not settle: not every property in China can take foreign guests at all. That is a booking question rather than a registration one, and our hotel booking guide deals with it.

If you stay anywhere else

You must register. This covers more than most people expect:

  • Staying with friends or family
  • A flat you own or rent
  • A university or company dormitory
  • Serviced or talent apartments
  • Any other non-hotel address

Residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan

Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan are part of China, and their residents register their accommodation when they come to the mainland, under a separate legal basis: Article 17 of the Provisional Measures for the Control of Chinese Citizens Entering and Leaving the Regions of Hong Kong and Macao for Personal Reasons, and Article 16 of the Measures for the Administration of Chinese Citizens Travelling to or from the Taiwan Region. The rules are otherwise the same as for foreign nationals, and the Shanghai guidance directs these residents to the city's system rather than the national one. See below.

The 24-hour rule

You have 24 hours from arriving at the address to complete the registration. The clock starts when you get to the place you are staying, not when you land in China.

The law puts the duty on either the visitor or the host, so if you are staying with a friend, the friend can file it. Keep the record afterwards. The registration is also an important basis for a later application for a residence permit.

Two systems, and which one is yours

Both are official, both produce a registration accepted by the public security authorities, and both can be reached from the same starting point. The difference is scope.

Online Accommodation Registration for International Visitors

外国人互联网住宿登记系统. The national system, marked applicable nationwide. It is the default for foreign nationals, and the one that was extended across the country on 21 September 2026. Register and verify an account first, then the path inside the platform is Services for Foreigners → Accommodation Registration, and a Registration Record Query shows what you filed. There is also a short operation video and a written guide, described below.

Self-service Accommodation Registration for Overseas Personnel

境外人员自助申报系统, marked applicable in Shanghai only. Shanghai has run this system since well before the national rollout. Foreign nationals in Shanghai may use it, and residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan are directed to it rather than to the national system. You sign in with an email address, upload the pages of your identification document, enter the residential address and the check-in date, and submit.

Who files it

Either the visitor or the host may be the person filing, and the host can be a Chinese citizen or a foreigner. There is one qualification that matters: the first registration at an address is made with the host's help, because the host knows the property details and the address has to be entered exactly right the first time.

After that, you can file for yourself in two situations:

  • You have registered on the platform before and are registering the same address again
  • You are staying in a property you own

With the Shanghai system, a resident of Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan files either personally or through the host.

What happens after you submit

In the national system, work through the form step by step and then query the record. In the Shanghai system the sequence is:

  1. Sign in with an email address to receive the Foreigner Accommodation Registration Form
  2. Upload the pages of your identification document and enter the residential address, the check-in date and the remaining details
  3. The system either clears the submission automatically or refers it to manual review. A manual review is answered within 24 hours
  4. Track the outcome under History Record, where an approved entry can be viewed and downloaded

Registering in person

The offline route has not closed. A "local public security window" means the household registration and other service windows at a police station, and the public security service windows inside community affairs service centres at sub-district or town level. Ask your local police station which is nearest.

Take the valid identification document of the person being registered. If someone else files on their behalf, or the host files, that person's identification document is required as well.

The registration form

How you get proof of registration depends on which route you used.

National system

The accommodation registration record is available to view in the system. When you apply for visa or residence permit services, or during an inspection, the electronic record is accepted in place of the paper form.

Shanghai system

Once the submission shows as approved, the system supplies the accommodation registration form, 境外人员住宿登记表, in electronic form for review and download.

Paper copy, if you want one

Go to any public security window nearby and ask for the printed Registration Form of Residence for Visitors from Overseas. It is printed and handed over after the record is verified.

Hotel stays

The hotel prints and provides the accommodation registration form for you.

When you do not need to register again

If you have already registered at an address, three situations do away with the repeat filing after you travel and come back:

A property you own in China

You live in a property that you or your spouse owns in China and return to it after travelling inside or outside China.

A habitual address, on a residence permit

You hold a residence permit or permanent residence permit and live at a habitual address, and you return to it after travel inside or outside China within the validity of the same permit.

A habitual address is somewhere you live, or plan to live, in China for the long term. Article 36 of the Regulations on Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners defines long term, or residence as residing in China for more than 180 days, and the immigration authority applies that threshold to the habitual-address exemption.

Residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan

You hold a valid Residence Permit for Hong Kong and Macao Residents or Residence Permit for Taiwan Residents, and you have already completed the registration. You do not register again while your place of residence is unchanged.

If something goes wrong

Three ways out, in the order most people use them:

  • Open the guide inside the system. The national system carries a written Online Accommodation Registration Guide covering who may register online, how a host files on someone's behalf, how to register from someone else's address and from your own, how to view and reuse a record, how to ask for help, and what to check after registering. The Shanghai system carries a Frequently Asked Questions panel, including what to do if a submitted form turns out to be wrong after it has been approved, and the correct way to write the address.
  • Call the 12367 immigration service hotline
  • Go to the exit-entry administration service window of the local public security organ, or your local police station, and ask for help filing in person

What happens if you do not register

Under Article 76 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, failing to register attracts a warning, and a fine of up to CNY 2,000 may also be imposed. The duty falls on the host as well as the visitor.

This is not a formality to leave until the end of a trip. Chinese hotels report every foreign guest automatically; a private address does not report itself, which is exactly the gap this registration fills.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register if I am staying at a friend's flat?
Yes. Any stay outside a hotel must be registered with the public security organ for that address within 24 hours after check-in — friends' homes, rented flats, student and company dormitories and serviced apartments all count. Since 21 September 2026 you can do it online instead of going to a police station.
Can I register online instead of going to a police station?
Yes, nationwide since 21 September 2026, through the National Immigration Administration platform: the services website at s.nia.gov.cn, the NIA 12367 app, or the NIA 12367 WeChat or Alipay mini program. In Shanghai the city's own Self-service Accommodation Registration for Overseas Personnel system is also available, at gaj.sh.gov.cn/crj/24hr and through the Suishenban app and mini programs. Online registration carries the same legal effect as registering in person, and the offline channel is still open.
Which system should I use, the national one or the Shanghai one?
Foreign nationals in Shanghai may use either. The Online Accommodation Registration for International Visitors system applies nationwide; the Self-service Accommodation Registration for Overseas Personnel system applies in Shanghai only. Residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan, which are part of China, are directed to the Shanghai system.
Who should file it — me or my host?
Either can be the person filing, and the host may be a Chinese citizen or a foreigner. The first registration at an address is made with the host's help, because the host knows the property details and the address has to be entered exactly. After that you can file for yourself if you have registered on the platform before and are filing the same address again, or if the address is a property you own.
I am staying in a hotel. Do I need to do anything?
No. Hotels register every guest under the hotel industry public security rules and report the record to the local public security organ. You only need to present a valid identification document, and the hotel prints the accommodation registration form for you. In Shanghai, any hotel can complete the procedure.
What do I get back after registering, and do I need the paper form?
Online registration leaves a record you can view and show. The national system accepts the electronic accommodation registration record when you apply for visa or residence permit services or during an inspection, so no paper form is needed. If you do want the printed Registration Form of Residence for Visitors from Overseas, any public security window nearby will print it after verifying the record, and registrations made in the Shanghai system can be downloaded from the History Record module once approved.
I left China and came back to the same address. Do I register again?
Not always. If you live in a property that you or your spouse owns in China, returning after travel inside or outside China does not require a new registration. If you hold a residence permit or permanent residence permit and live at a habitual address, the same applies within the validity of that permit. A habitual address means living in China for more than 180 days. Residents of Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan holding a valid Residence Permit for Hong Kong and Macao Residents or for Taiwan Residents who have already registered do not need to register again while their place of residence is unchanged.
What happens if I do not register?
Under Article 76 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, failing to register attracts a warning, and a fine of up to CNY 2,000 may also be imposed. The obligation applies to the host as well as the visitor, so it is worth filing inside the 24-hour window.
