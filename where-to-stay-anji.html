Where to Stay in Anji

Bamboo, rivers and a county the size of a small country. Five bases, compared by what they are actually good for.

Anji is large enough that the wrong base turns a weekend into a driving lesson. Pick the valley first, the hotel second.

At a glance

  • Five bases, from ¥250 in Dipu to ¥1,800 at Lingfeng.
  • Shanghai Hongqiao to Anji: 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 40, ¥92 to ¥111.
  • Direct trains are limited, so the return service decides the trip.
  • A car is needed for Tianhuangping, Shanchuan and Baofu.
  • Two nights for one valley; three to move between valleys.
  • Late spring and autumn are the settled months.

What is the best area to stay in Anji?

Anji is a county of 1,900 square kilometres, so the valley decides the trip. Families go to Lingfeng for the pools and parks, walkers to Tianhuangping, river groups to Baofu, and anyone chasing views to Shanchuan. Dipu is the practical base for late arrivals and for travellers without a car.

The five bases

  • First visit, without a car: Dipu, the county seat. Restaurants, supermarkets, a taxi rank and the widest choice of ordinary hotels.
  • Families with children: Lingfeng, the resort zone north of the town, built around theme parks and large hotels with pools.
  • Bamboo and cooler air: Tianhuangping, the mountain village below the pumped-storage plant, ringed by the densest bamboo in the county.
  • Views and open grassland: Shanchuan, high on the ridge, where the cloud grassland sits above the tree line.
  • Rivers and rafting: Baofu, in the south-west, with shallow rivers, stone bridges and guesthouses that open onto water.
Rule of thumb: Anji is a county, not a village. Two bases in one weekend means three hours in the car. Choose one.

Dipu: the county seat

Not the reason you came, but the base that makes everything else easier.

What the county town solves

  • Logistics: the railway station sits on the edge of town, so a late train does not become a late drive up a mountain road. The delta guide compares journey times from Shanghai.
  • Food: a strong choice of ordinary restaurants, plus supermarkets and pharmacies. In a rural county this matters more than it sounds.
  • Price: a third to a half of resort rates, which frees the budget for a better room elsewhere on a longer trip.
Be aware: you are in a working town. There is no view, and the drive to the bamboo takes 40 minutes or more.
• • •

Lingfeng: the family resort zone

Purpose-built for children, with the pools and the parks to prove it.

Built for children

  • Facilities: a dense cluster of hotels with indoor pools, playrooms and grounds, which is what a rainy day needs.
  • Parks: the theme park and the big garden-park next door are both a short drive away, so a day out does not require planning.
  • Rooms: family suites and connecting rooms are standard here, unlike in the village guesthouses further south.
Be aware: weekend rates double, and the zone has more car park than character. If you want bamboo at the window, this is not it.
• • •

Tianhuangping: bamboo and cool air

The classic Anji base, and still the best one for walking.

Bamboo at the window

  • Setting: the village sits at altitude below the pumped-storage station, with bamboo on every slope and a temperature a few degrees below the county town.
  • Trails: the bamboo sea and the reservoir road are close, and several guesthouses are within walking distance of a trailhead.
  • Guesthouses: a mature cluster of family-run houses, most with dinner included and a terrace facing the valley.
Watch for: the road up is winding and unlit. Do not arrive after dark on a first visit, and confirm the driver will take you to the door.
• • •

Shanchuan: the high ridge

For views and open sky, at the cost of distance from everything.

Above the tree line

  • Altitude: the grassland sits above the tree line, with grass, wind and long views rather than bamboo tunnels.
  • Photography: sunrise and cloud inversions are the reason people come up here, and staying on the ridge removes the pre-dawn drive.
  • Summer: cooler nights than the valley, which matters in July and August.
Be aware: supply is thin, prices are high for what you get, and the road closes in heavy weather. Have a fallback in Tianhuangping.
• • •

Baofu: rivers and rafting

The south-west valleys, where the water is the point.

The water is the point

  • Water: clear, shallow rivers with stone bridges, swimming holes and rafting sections nearby.
  • Groups: guesthouses with courtyards and kitchens, priced per house rather than per room, suit six to ten people.
  • Quiet: far enough from the resort zone that weekends feel like weekdays, except in August.
  • Combine with: Moganshan is two hours north, for a mountain after the rivers.

How do you get to Anji?

Shanghai Hongqiao to Anji takes 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 40 for ¥92 to ¥111, but direct trains are limited to a handful a day, so the return service decides the trip. From Anji station, Dipu is 15 to 25 minutes by taxi for under ¥60; Tianhuangping is 40 to 50 minutes for ¥100 to ¥140.

When should you go?

Late spring and autumn are the reliable windows: dry, clear and settled. August is hot in town and pleasant on the ridge, which is why the pools and the rafting are busiest then. Weekdays in May and October give you the valleys without the queues.

By season

  • March to May: bamboo shoots, green hills and good walking weather. Rain is frequent but short.
  • June to August: hot and humid in Dipu, five degrees cooler on the ridge. Rafting peaks, and so do weekend prices.
  • September to November: dry, clear and the most settled months of the year. Book two weeks ahead for weekends.
  • December to February: cold, often grey, and very quiet. Some guesthouses close between the New Year and the spring festival.

Booking practicalities

Four things worth confirming before you pay.

Rail services are limited

Direct trains from Shanghai run a handful of times a day, not the twenty-minute frequency of the Suzhou line. Check the return service before you book the room, not after.

Foreign guest registration

Most resorts and established hotels are licensed to receive foreign passports. Village guesthouses are hit and miss. Ask directly and keep the written reply, especially for cheaper listings.

Dinner is often the only dinner

In Tianhuangping, Shanchuan and Baofu there may be no restaurant within walking distance. Most guesthouses cook, but they need to know by mid-afternoon and they expect cash or a QR payment.

Driving at night is not a plan

Mountain roads are narrow, unlit and shared with scooters. Arrive in daylight, and do not schedule a late transfer on the day you land.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions that come up before booking.

How do I get to Anji from Shanghai?

Direct high-speed trains run from Shanghai Hongqiao in 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 40 minutes for ¥92 to ¥111, but only a few times a day. By car it is two and a half to three hours, which gives more flexibility for the valleys.

Do I need a car in Anji?

Yes for Tianhuangping, Shanchuan and Baofu. Ride-hailing exists but supply is thin in the valleys, especially after dark, and some guesthouses sit three kilometres from the nearest junction. Dipu and Lingfeng can be done without one.

Is Anji good for families?

Yes, particularly the Lingfeng resort zone, which has pools, parks and family rooms within a short drive. The bamboo villages suit older children who can walk, and Baofu's rivers work best for groups in summer, when the water is low.

How many days do I need in Anji?

Two nights for one valley, three for two. The county is large and the roads are slow, so a day trip from Hangzhou sees very little of it. With one night, base yourself in Dipu or Lingfeng and stay put.

When is the best time to visit Anji?

Late spring and autumn. Summer works if you stay high on the Shanchuan ridge, where nights are cooler than in the valley. Winter is quiet and often grey, and some guesthouses close between the New Year and spring.
