Day Trips from Beijing

The Great Wall, the Summer Palace, the Ming Tombs, a water town at night and a city by the sea. The best escapes from Beijing, ranked with times, prices and honest advice.

Beijing is a capital of enormous distances and enormous history, and some of its best days are the ones spent leaving it. The Wall is the obvious one. It is not the only one.
• • •

What are the best day trips from Beijing?

The Great Wall is the essential one: Mutianyu for the best balance (90 minutes by car, ¥45 admission, cable car and toboggan), or Badaling for the easiest rail access (22 minutes by high-speed rail, ¥40). The Summer Palace is a classic half-day by Metro on Line 4. The Ming Tombs pair naturally with a wall visit. Gubei Water Town at the foot of Simatai is the one worth staying for, especially after dark. Tianjin is 30 minutes away by high-speed rail for a different flavour entirely.

One trip, one destination. Beijing distances punish the double-dip.

The Great Wall: the essential day

The reason most people come to Beijing. Choose your section deliberately.

Mutianyu - the best balance

  • 90 minutes from central Beijing by car or the public tourist bus. Admission ¥45, cable car ¥100 round trip, toboggan for the way down.
  • Restored, genuinely beautiful, and moderately crowded compared with Badaling. Book tickets online the night before via the WeChat mini-programme or Trip.com.
  • A Didi from the city runs roughly ¥300-450 each way - with two or three people it beats the tour bus for flexibility.

Badaling - the easy rail option

  • The high-speed rail from Beijing North to Badaling station takes 22 minutes and costs ¥21-28. This is the fastest door-to-door option there is.
  • Admission ¥40, cable car ¥140 round trip. Wheelchair and pram friendly - the most accessible section.
  • The trade-off is the crowds: Badaling is the section on every Chinese school tour and every group itinerary.

The Summer Palace: the graceful half-day

15 km northwest of the centre, and a different pace entirely.

Why it works

  • China's most beautiful imperial garden: Kunming Lake, the Long Corridor's painted ceilings, and the Tower of Buddhist Incense looking back over the water.
  • Metro Line 4 to Xiyuan station, then a ten-minute walk to the East Gate. Forty minutes from the centre.
  • Admission ¥30 (grounds) or ¥60 (combined ticket). A boat across the lake and the Long Corridor walk fill a comfortable three hours.
Extend it: the ruins of the Old Summer Palace (圆明园), a fifteen-minute walk or one Metro stop away, turn the half-day into a full day. Entrance ¥10, and the ruined marble arches are a genuinely moving sight.
• • •

The Ming Tombs: history with the wall

Thirteen Ming emperors lie 50 km northwest of the city.

How to see them

  • The Dingling (定陵) tomb is the excavated one - the underground palace of the Wanli Emperor is the centrepiece, admission ¥60.
  • The Sacred Way (神道), a processional avenue lined with stone animals and officials, is the photographic heart of the site.
  • They combine naturally with a Great Wall visit on the same day, since both lie to the northwest of the city - a morning at the wall and an afternoon among the tombs works well with a private car.
• • •

Gubei Water Town: the night escape

Two hours from the city, at the foot of Simatai - the one worth staying for.

Why it works

  • A rebuilt canal town on the Miyun reservoir, set directly beneath Simatai's unrestored wall.
  • Combined entry ¥140; the water town lights up at night and the wall above it follows.
  • Night tickets for Simatai are ¥160 and the illuminated wall is the single most photographed view in northern China.
  • It is a two-hour drive each way - book a night in one of the town's courtyard hotels rather than turning it around in a day.
• • •

Tianjin: a different China by rail

Thirty minutes away, and a genuinely different city.

Why it works

  • The high-speed rail from Beijing South to Tianjin runs every ten minutes or so; ¥54-66 each way, about 30 minutes.
  • The Hai River waterfront and the century-old Five Great Avenues (五大道) - the largest concession-era quarter in China - are the draw.
  • Wander the food streets around the ancient culture street (古文化街) for local snacks, and walk the waterfront at dusk.
  • Best as a change of pace after the Beijing classics, not instead of them.
• • •

Practical notes

Trains and tickets

  • Book wall tickets online - the WeChat mini-programme or Trip.com - the night before. There are rarely same-day walk-up tickets in season.
  • For rail trips, buy your return when you arrive; evening trains sell out on weekends.
  • Foreign passports work on 12306 and Trip.com - full steps in our Train Booking Guide.

Money and payment

  • Alipay covers tickets, meals and the odd temple donation. Keep ¥200-300 cash for the smallest vendors.
  • Some water-town and tomb tickets are cash-only at the gate.

Getting there on time

  • Allow 30 minutes by Metro to Beijing South station, 25 to Beijing North.
  • Bring water year-round - Beijing is dry - and a coat in winter; the wall and the lake have no shelter.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best day trips from Beijing?

The Great Wall is the essential day trip - Mutianyu for the best balance, Badaling for the easiest rail access. The Summer Palace and the Ming Tombs are classic half-to-full day additions. Gubei Water Town pairs a night with Simatai's illuminated wall. Tianjin is 30 minutes away by high-speed rail for a change of pace.

Should I visit Mutianyu or Badaling for a Great Wall day trip?

Mutianyu for a first visit: 90 minutes from the city, genuinely beautiful, moderately crowded, with a cable car and a toboggan. Badaling is the easiest to reach - 22 minutes on the high-speed rail from Beijing North - but it is the most crowded section. Full comparison of all seven sections in the EVENTURUS Great Wall Guide.

Is Tianjin worth a day trip from Beijing?

Yes, as a change of pace rather than a bucket-list stop. Tianjin is 30 minutes by high-speed rail and offers the Hai River waterfront, century-old foreign concessions and some of China's best food streets. It is at its most worthwhile if you have seen the Beijing classics already.

How far is the Summer Palace from central Beijing?

About 15 kilometres northwest of the centre - roughly 40 minutes by Metro on Line 4 to Xiyuan station. The palace and its lake need a full half-day to see properly; combined with the nearby ruins of the Old Summer Palace it becomes a full day.
