China High-Speed Train Seat Guide

Compare seat classes at a glance. Clean diagrams + real cabin photos.

Good to know: All high-speed train seats face forward. Trains are very punctual. Boarding gates close 3-5 minutes before departure. Tickets are electronic. Your foreign passport works at staffed check-in gates, and a growing number of major stations have passport-reading automatic gates.
Price update, May 2026: From 26 May 2026 the published fare ceiling on the Beijing–Shanghai line rose by 20%. Fares you actually pay were unchanged at launch; second class on this route currently runs about ¥576–673 depending on the train, with the new ceiling near ¥800 for peak departures. Off-peak and slower services still carry discounts. Check the Transit Matrix for route-specific pricing.

EVENTURUS tips for booking

  1. Book early. Tickets go on sale 15 days ahead, counting the day of travel. For Golden Week or Chinese New Year, book the moment they release.
  2. Use Trip.com or the 12306 app in English. Both accept foreign passports.
  3. Choose window seats (A or F) for the best views. A = window on the 3-seat side, F = window on the 2-seat side.
  4. 1st class is the sweet spot for trips over 3 hours. Only 1.7× the cost but significantly more comfortable.
  5. Business class is worth it for routes like Shanghai–Beijing (4.5h) if you want to sleep or work in peace.
  6. Choose your sleeper wisely. Hard sleeper (硬卧) is open-plan with 6 bunks, best left to backpackers. Soft sleeper (软卧) has a lockable 4-berth cabin; buy all 4 bunks for a private room. HSR sleeper (动卧) runs at 250 km/h on D trains, covering Beijing–Shanghai overnight in around 12 hours, with USB at every bed.
  7. Bring snacks. Food carts come through but selection is limited. Pack snacks and a drink.
