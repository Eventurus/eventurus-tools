The Palace Museum

故宫博物院 (Gùgōng Bówùyuàn)
Beijing 北京
HistoryArchitectureCulture & HeritageArt
The world's largest imperial palace complex - home to 24 emperors across the Ming and Qing dynasties. A UNESCO World Heritage site housing over 1.8 million artefacts. Not just a museum; it's the centre of gravity for Chinese civilisation.
08:30 - 17:00 (Apr - Oct); 08:30 - 16:30 (Nov - Mar). Closed Mondays except public holidays.
¥60 (peak) / ¥40 (off-peak). Extra ¥10 for Treasure Gallery, ¥10 for Clock Gallery.
Free on International Museum Day (18 May) - advance booking still required. Free for children under 120 cm.
Book: WeChat mini-program '故宫博物院' or book at intl.dpm.org.cn with email + passport number (no Chinese phone needed). Book up to 7 days ahead. Releases at 20:00 Beijing time - tickets for peak dates vanish within minutes.
Enter via the Meridian Gate (south entrance, nearest metro: Tian'anmen East). Exit at the north gate for Jingshan Park's panoramic rooftop view. Audio guides in 40 languages available at ¥40. Allow 3 - 5 hours minimum; a full day is better.

National Museum of China

国家博物馆 (Guójiā Bówùguǎn)
Beijing 北京
HistoryCulture & HeritageArtArchaeology
China's flagship museum on the eastern flank of Tian'anmen Square. Covers 5,000 years of Chinese history through staggering archaeological finds - oracle bones, Shang bronzes, Han jade burial suits, and the famous Simuwu Ding.
09:00 - 17:00 (last entry 16:00). Closed Mondays.
Free. Passport required for entry.
Always free - but booking is mandatory.
Book: WeChat mini-program '国家博物馆'. Book 1 - 7 days ahead. Enter your passport details. Morning slots (09:00 - 13:30) and afternoon slots (13:30 - 16:00) - the morning ones go first.
Security is airport-level strict - no lighters, no large bags, no tripods. The basement 'Ancient China' gallery alone deserves 2 - 3 hours. English labels are decent but audio guides make a big difference.

Free. Passport required. Some special exhibitions charge ¥30 - 50.

798 Art Zone

798艺术区 (798 Yìshù Qū)
Beijing 北京
ArtCulture & HeritageArchitecture
Not a single museum but an entire Bauhaus factory complex turned contemporary art district. Beijing's answer to Bushwick or Berlin-Mitte - galleries, studios, concept shops, and cafes spread across decommissioned East German-designed electronics factories.
Galleries typically 10:00 - 18:00. Most closed Mondays. The district itself is always open.
Free entry to the district. Individual galleries free; some special exhibitions ¥20 - 100.
Most permanent collections are free. Check individual gallery websites for special exhibition pricing.
Book: No booking needed for the district. Major galleries like UCCA require tickets for special exhibitions - book via their WeChat mini-programs.
Come on a weekend afternoon when the district is alive. Start at UCCA and wander. Great coffee at Voyage Coffee. Allow 3 - 4 hours to explore properly. Metro Line 14 to Wangjing South.

Emperor Qinshihuang's Mausoleum Site Museum

秦始皇兵马俑博物馆 (Qínshǐhuáng Bīngmǎyǒng Bówùguǎn)
Xi'an 西安
HistoryArchaeologyMilitaryCulture & Heritage
The Terracotta Warriors. Three pits containing over 8,000 life-sized soldiers, horses, and chariots - each with unique facial features - buried to guard China's first emperor in the afterlife. One of the greatest archaeological discoveries of the 20th century.
08:30 - 18:30 (Mar - Nov); 08:30 - 18:00 (Dec - Feb). Open daily.
¥120 (peak Mar - Nov) / ¥90 (off-peak). Audio guide ¥30 - 40 extra.
Free for seniors over 65 (Chinese citizens only). Children under 12 free.
Book: WeChat mini-program '秦始皇帝陵博物院'. English tickets available on Trip.com (search 'Terracotta Warriors'). Book at least 1 day ahead. On-site ticket windows exist but queues are long.
Go early (08:30 opening) to beat the crowds - by 10:00 it's packed. Visit in reverse order: Pit 3 → Pit 2 → Pit 1 to build anticipation. The museum is 40km east of Xi'an; take the tourist bus No. 5 (306) from Xi'an Railway Station. Allow 3 - 4 hours.

Shaanxi History Museum

陕西历史博物馆 (Shǎnxī Lìshǐ Bówùguǎn)
Xi'an 西安
HistoryArchaeologyCulture & HeritageArt
Xi'an's crown jewel alongside the Terracotta Warriors. Covers 1.15 million years of history from the Lantian Man to the Tang Dynasty, when Xi'an (then Chang'an) was the largest city in the world. The Tang dynasty murals and gold artefacts are breathtaking.
09:00 - 17:30 (summer); 09:00 - 17:00 (winter). Closed Mondays.
Free for the main galleries. Tang mural hall ¥270 extra. Tang treasures hall ¥30 extra.
Always free for main galleries - but the ticket is possibly the hardest to get in China.
Book: WeChat mini-program '陕西历史博物馆'. This is one of the most difficult museum tickets in China - slots release at specific times and disappear in under a minute. Book 3 - 5 days ahead and be ready at the release hour. Try the less-crowded Qin-Han Museum branch as a backup.
If you can't get a ticket (common), the Xi'an Museum is an excellent, much easier alternative. The paid galleries are also easier to book. Metro Line 2 to Xiaozhai. Audio guide strongly recommended - ¥30, decent English.

Housed within the grounds of the Small Wild Goose Pagoda (a 1,300-year-old Tang dynasty pagoda), this museum offers a quieter, more intimate look at Xi'an's history. Excellent Buddhist art collection and Tang dynasty artefacts - and far less crowded than Shaanxi History Museum.

A fantastic Plan B if you can't get into the Shaanxi History Museum. The pagoda grounds are lovely for a stroll. Metro Line 2 to Nanshaomen. Combine with the nearby Shaanxi History Museum (if you got tickets) for a full history day.

Banpo Museum

半坡博物馆 (Bànpō Bówùguǎn)
Xi'an 西安
ArchaeologyHistory
Built directly over a 6,000-year-old Neolithic village site. The first large-scale museum built on an archaeological excavation in China. You literally walk over the excavation pit and see the remains of houses, pottery kilns, and burial sites of the Yangshao culture.
08:00 - 17:30 (summer); 08:30 - 17:00 (winter). Open daily.
¥65 (summer) / ¥45 (winter).
Free for seniors over 65 and children under 6. No general free day.
Book: WeChat mini-program '西安半坡博物馆'. Same-day booking usually fine. On-site ticket purchase available.
A short ride from the Terracotta Warriors direction - combine them if you have a driver. The museum is compact, 1 - 1.5 hours is enough. English signage is improving. Fascinating if you're into prehistory; skip if you have limited time in Xi'an.

Xi'an Beilin Museum (Stele Forest)

China's oldest museum (founded 1087), built around a collection of stone stelae - massive stone tablets inscribed with classical Chinese texts, calligraphy, and historical records. The 'stone library' of Chinese civilisation, housed in a serene Confucian temple complex.

¥65 (summer) / ¥50 (winter).
Free for seniors over 65 (Chinese citizens). Children under 6 free.
Book: WeChat mini-program '西安碑林博物馆'. Same-day booking usually fine. On-site tickets available.
A hidden gem that most foreign tourists skip. If you're interested in Chinese writing, calligraphy, or religious history, this is essential. Located near the South Gate of the city wall - combine with a walk along the wall. Allow 1.5 - 2 hours.

Sanxingdui Museum

三星堆博物馆 (Sānxīngduī Bówùguǎn)
Chengdu 成都
ArchaeologyHistoryCulture & HeritageArt
One of the most astonishing archaeological discoveries anywhere - a previously unknown Bronze Age civilisation that existed 3,000 - 5,000 years ago in Sichuan, producing enormous bronze masks with bulging eyes, gold foil masks, and a 4-metre-tall bronze 'tree of life'. Nothing like it exists elsewhere in China.
08:30 - 18:00 (peak); 08:30 - 17:00 (off-peak). Open daily.
¥72. New museum (opened 2023) included in ticket.
Free for seniors over 60 and active military. Children under 1.3m free.
Book: WeChat mini-program '三星堆博物馆'. Book 3 - 5 days ahead. English tickets available via Trip.com. The new 2023 museum has dramatically increased capacity but it's still very popular.
It's in Guanghan, about 50km north of Chengdu. Take the high-speed train from Chengdu East to Guanghan North (20 min), then a taxi. The new 2023 museum is world-class - allow 3 - 4 hours. The bronze masks will haunt you (in a good way).

The world's premier giant panda conservation and research centre. Not a traditional museum, but a 100-hectare park with panda enclosures, a panda museum, a panda hospital, and the famous 'nursery' where baby pandas nap in incubators. The red panda section is equally charming.

HistoryMilitary
A harrowing, essential memorial to the 300,000 civilians killed during the 1937 Japanese occupation of Nanjing. The architecture - dark corridors, gravel paths (symbolising mass graves), walls of names - is as powerful as the exhibits. Deeply moving. Not for young children.

Free.
Always free.

09:00 - 17:00. Closed Mondays.
Free.
Always free.

The museum is 45 minutes northwest of Hangzhou by metro (Line 2 to Liangzhu). Combined with the archaeological park, it's a full-day trip. The architecture photographs beautifully in late afternoon light. Allow 2 hours for the museum, 3+ if doing the park.
