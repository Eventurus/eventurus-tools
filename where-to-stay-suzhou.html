Where to Stay in Suzhou

The old town, the lake and the water towns, compared by what each one is good for. Twenty-five minutes from Shanghai, so the base matters more than the journey.

Suzhou rewards the traveller who stays inside the moat. The gardens are quiet at eight in the morning, and the city is a fifteen-minute walk from anywhere worth being.

At a glance

  • Five bases, from ¥400 in Tongli to ¥1,800 on Jinji Lake.
  • Shanghai Hongqiao to Suzhou: 23 to 30 minutes, ¥31 to ¥46 second class.
  • First visit: Pingjiang Road, inside the moat.
  • Arrive at Suzhou station, not Suzhou North, for the old town.
  • Two nights covers the gardens, the canals and one water town.
  • Tongli is 50 minutes away by metro and tram.

What is the best area to stay in Suzhou?

For a first visit, stay inside the moat at Pingjiang Road: the gardens, the canals and the museum are a walk apart, and the metro reaches the rest. Jinji Lake suits business trips and anyone who wants space rather than a courtyard. Tongli is worth one night, not the whole trip.

The five bases

  • First visit, gardens and canals: Pingjiang Road and the old town north, inside the moat, walkable to the Humble Administrator's Garden and the museum.
  • Shopping and convenience: Guanqian Street, the commercial core, with metro, restaurants and the densest cluster of mid-range hotels.
  • Gardens and the west side: Shantang and Shilu, along the canal near the Lingering Garden, quieter in the evening than Guanqian.
  • Business and lake views: SIP and Jinji Lake, the modern east side, with towers, malls and the newest hotel rooms in the city.
  • A water-town night: Tongli, 50 minutes out by metro and tram, for one night rather than a whole trip.
Rule of thumb: two nights in the old town beats four nights split between the old town and the lake. Suzhou is compact; you do not need to move.

Pingjiang Road and the old town north

The default for a first visit, and the only base where the main sights are a walk apart.

What the old town gives you

  • Sights: the Humble Administrator's Garden, the Suzhou Museum and the Lion Grove sit within twenty minutes on foot, and Pingjiang Road itself runs along a canal the whole way.
  • Atmosphere: whitewashed walls, humpback bridges and lanes too narrow for cars. In the early morning it belongs to residents rather than visitors.
  • Transport: Metro Line 4 stops at Beisita, one stop from Suzhou station, which makes arrival with luggage simple.
Watch for: Pingjiang Road is the most photographed lane in the city by late morning. Ask for a room on a side alley, not the main street.
• • •

Guanqian Street: the practical centre

Less romantic, but the easiest place to be based if you are travelling by metro and eating out.

The trade-off

  • Connections: Line 4 and Line 1 cross here, putting the old town, the station and Jinji Lake within a single change.
  • Food: the highest density of restaurants in the city, from noodle shops to the old establishments serving Suzhou classics.
  • Value: business hotels discount at weekends, when the corporate trade leaves. Friday and Saturday can be the cheapest nights.
Be aware: the pedestrian street is commercial and loud. It is a base, not a reason to come.
• • •

Shantang and Shilu: the west canals

A quieter old-town edge, better for gardens than for nightlife.

Quieter canals, same convenience

  • Gardens: the Lingering Garden and the West Garden Temple are close, and both are less crowded than the Humble Administrator's.
  • Canal: Shantang Street runs along the water for a kilometre, lit at night, with teahouses rather than bars.
  • Rooms: a handful of courtyard hotels in restored houses, with fewer than twenty rooms each.
• • •

SIP and Jinji Lake: the modern east

For business trips and for anyone who wants a large room with a lake view rather than a courtyard.

Space, a desk and a lake view

  • Rooms: the newest hotel stock in the city, with lake views and proper desks.
  • Transport: Metro Line 1 connects the lake to the old town in about twenty-five minutes.
  • Evening: the lakeside walk, the opera house and a mall culture that runs late, which the old town does not.
Be aware: this is a new city. If your trip is gardens and canals, you will spend 40 minutes a day in transit.
• • •

Tongli: the water-town night

Worth one night, not four. The town empties at five, the canals belong to residents, and there is not enough left to fill a second day.

One night, empty lanes

  • Access: Metro Line 4 runs from Suzhou station to Tongli in about fifty minutes, then a short tram to the gate. No car needed.
  • Evening: day visitors leave, lanterns come on, and the lanes become walkable. This is the reason to sleep here.
  • Guesthouses: courtyard houses inside the old town, simple and cheap, with the occasional restored merchant villa.
  • Second water town: Wuzhen is the more restored alternative, 90 minutes away by road, if one canal town is not enough.
Watch for: tickets and re-entry rules vary by town. If you plan to leave for dinner and come back, confirm that your ticket allows it.

Which station should you book?

Suzhou, not Suzhou North. The main station is two kilometres from the old town; the north station is fifteen.

The difference matters

  • Suzhou station: on the edge of the historic centre, Metro Lines 2 and 4, ten minutes by taxi to Pingjiang Road.
  • Suzhou North: a new station in Xiangcheng, about thirty minutes by metro from the gardens. Book it only if your train goes there or your hotel is on that side.
  • Suzhou Industrial Park: useful for SIP hotels, pointless for the old town.
Booking tip: apps default to Suzhou North when you type Suzhou. Check the arrival station before you pay, not after. Continuing south? Hangzhou is 70 to 100 minutes by rail.

How do you get to Suzhou?

Shanghai Hongqiao to Suzhou takes 23 to 30 minutes for ¥31 to ¥46 in second class, and the trains run closer to a commuter service than an intercity timetable. Add 10 to 15 minutes and ¥14 to ¥18 by taxi from Suzhou station to Pingjiang Road.

When should you go?

Spring and autumn are the seasons to aim for, and early morning is the real answer in every season. The gardens are small and the lanes are narrow, so crowds decide the experience more than weather does. Two nights is enough to see the old town without hurrying.

By season

  • March to May: the gardens in flower, mild temperatures, and the heaviest weekend crowds of the year.
  • June to August: hot and humid, with occasional heavy rain. Early mornings are still pleasant and the gardens are quieter.
  • September to November: a good combination of weather and light. Book two weeks ahead for a weekend in October.
  • December to February: cold and grey, but the gardens are almost empty and hotel rates fall sharply outside the New Year week.

Booking practicalities

Four small things that decide whether the trip works.

Gardens need timed entry

The Humble Administrator's Garden and the museum both limit numbers. Reserve a morning slot in advance, especially at weekends, and carry your passport, which is checked against the booking.

Foreign guest registration

Most international and upper mid-range hotels are licensed to receive foreign passports. Small courtyard guesthouses in the old town sometimes cannot. Confirm in writing before paying.

The old town is not car-friendly

Many lanes are pedestrian only and taxis cannot reach the door. Ask the property where the nearest drop-off point is, and how far you will carry the bag.

Weekends are the expensive nights

Suzhou is a weekend city for Shanghai. Business hotels in Guanqian and SIP discount on Friday and Saturday, while courtyard hotels in the old town charge a premium for the same nights.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions that come up before booking.

How far is Suzhou from Shanghai?

About 25 minutes by high-speed train from Shanghai Hongqiao to Suzhou station, with services every ten to twenty minutes and second-class fares from ¥31. It is faster than crossing central Shanghai, which is why day trips work so well.

Which station should I arrive at in Suzhou?

Suzhou station, on the edge of the old town and two kilometres from Pingjiang Road. It sits on Metro Lines 2 and 4. Suzhou North is fifteen kilometres out and adds about thirty minutes by metro, so check the arrival station before paying.

Is Suzhou worth staying overnight?

Yes. The gardens are calm before nine and the canals are at their best after five, and neither is available on a day trip. Two nights lets you see the old town properly and add Tongli without rushing the return.

Can I visit Tongli from Suzhou without a car?

Yes. Metro Line 4 runs from Suzhou station to Tongli in about fifty minutes, then a short tram covers the last stretch, for under ¥10 in total. Staying the night is what makes it worthwhile, because the lanes empty after five.

Do Suzhou hotels accept foreign passports?

Most international and upper mid-range hotels are licensed to. Registration is granted per property, so small courtyard guesthouses in the old town are less consistent. Ask in writing before you pay and keep the reply, particularly for cheaper listings.
