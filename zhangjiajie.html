Zhangjiajie: how many days, and which park you actually want

Two days buys you one park done properly. Three buys both. Here is what each morning costs in travel, tickets and queueing.

The answer Two parks Tickets Three days Two or four Getting there Where to stay Weather What goes wrong Questions

The short answer

Two days for one park. Three for both. Anything less is a coach tour.

Two days — Wulingyuan only, two mornings in the pillars, Tianmen left for next time. Three days — the usual shape: two mornings in Wulingyuan, one in Tianmen, sleeping in two different towns. Four days — add the glass bridge at the Grand Canyon or a slow morning on the Yangjiajie ridge.

Zhangjiajie is not one park. It is two separately ticketed parks about 40 km apart, plus a glass bridge that belongs to neither. Wulingyuan holds the sandstone pillars on every poster. Tianmen Mountain stands at the edge of the city, with a cableway that runs 7,455 metres up to it.

Neither fits into half a day. Wulingyuan rewards two mornings and punishes one. Tianmen takes five to seven hours from the moment you join the cableway queue.

2.5 days buys Wulingyuan and a rushed morning at Tianmen with a train to catch. It works. It is not restful.

If Zhangjiajie is one stop on a longer route, our guide to how many days to give each Chinese city sets it beside Guilin, Chengdu and Chongqing.

Two parks, not one

The names overlap and the tickets do not.

Inside Wulingyuan the four parts are joined by green shuttle buses, which is why the ¥236 ticket rather than the ¥165 one is the sensible buy: the buses are the only way between Yuanjiajie, Tianzi and the valley floor.

The pillar you have seen in the film is signposted Qiankun zhu (乾坤柱) in Yuanjiajie. The Avatar name arrived after the film and is a nickname; nothing on the park's own signage uses it.

If you only have one morning: Yuanjiajie, entered from the East gate and lifted by the Bailong Elevator. That is the photograph, and it costs the least walking.

Tianmen is the one to drop on a short trip. It is a good mountain with a spectacular lift, but it is a different day, in a different town, on a different ticket.

How the ticket and the shuttle work

One price for the gate, four days to use it, and a queue for everything that moves.

The Wulingyuan gate ticket is ¥165 and the version that includes the park shuttle buses is ¥236, both valid for four consecutive days. Insurance is ¥3 and voluntary. A discounted combined ticket is ¥119, for the age and student categories the gate checks on the day.

Those figures come from the Hunan price notice that took effect on 1 January 2025 and runs for five years. The shuttle bus price was reset separately from 1 January 2026, which is why the older ¥225 and ¥240 figures still circulating are out of date.

Two things changed recently and catch people out. Entry is now by timed slot as well as by ticket: you book a morning or an afternoon and the slots are released about a week ahead, so a same-day arrival in the peak season is a gamble. And a passport number, not a Chinese ID, is what you type into the booking.

Wulingyuan's five gates are not interchangeable. East at Biaozhimen sits beside Wulingyuan town, South serves the Forest Park and Golden Whip Stream, North serves Tianzi, West serves Yangjiajie, and Central is the quiet one.

Three days, in order

Two mornings in the pillars, one on Tianmen, two beds.

Day one · arrive and walk the valley

Train into Zhangjiajie West, shuttle to Wulingyuan town, bags dropped by 13:00. Afternoon: the South gate, then Golden Whip Stream on the flat for 7.5 km towards Shuiraosimen, two and a half hours at an easy pace. Bailong Elevator up, shuttle bus back to the East gate. Early night.

Day two · Yuanjiajie and Tianzi

East gate for the first slot, Bailong up, then the shuttle to Yuanjiajie for the Qiankun pillar and the natural bridge before 10:00, when the coaches arrive. Shuttle across to Tianzi in the afternoon for the peak forest, then the cable car down. This is the day the trip is for, and it needs the whole of it.

Day three · Tianmen, then out

Sleep in the city on the second night, within walking distance of the Tianmen cableway station. First cableway around 08:00, the cliff walkways, the cave and the 999 steps, back at the bottom by 14:00. The station is ten minutes away, which is why this day goes last.

Golden Whip Stream, the direction that works: walk it downhill from the south towards Shuiraosimen, with the water on your left. The opposite direction climbs gently for two hours and ends far from the town's hotels.

That ordering solves the luggage problem too: one bag move, Wulingyuan to the city, on the morning of day three, before the cableway. Our train booking guide covers passports and collection at the station.

Two days, or four

Where to cut, and where the extra day goes.

Two days means one park. Keep Wulingyuan, take the East gate both mornings, ride the elevator both ways, and put Tianmen out of your mind. Trying to add a Tianmen morning to a two-day trip leaves three hours there and one of them in a queue.

Four days adds either the glass bridge or a third Wulingyuan morning. The bridge is a half day with the transfer and is booked by slot. The third morning is better spent on the Yangjiajie ridge, which coach groups skip and which holds a wall of pillars rather than single ones.

Huangshizhai is the other candidate for a spare morning: 3,800 steps up, or the cableway, and the best view back over the valley. Beyond four days you are walking rather than looking, which suits some trips and not most.

Two stations carry the name and they are 10 km apart. Zhangjiajie West is the high-speed one; Zhangjiajie station is the conventional one, and a ticket naming it will not get you on a high-speed train.

From the West station to Wulingyuan: the official shuttle is ¥13 and takes about 40 minutes, taxis ask ¥80 to ¥120, and shared cars run about ¥20 a seat. The shuttle drops at the bus station, which is 2 km from the East gate, so add a local bus or a short taxi.

Hehua airport is 5 km from the city centre and about 8 km from the Tianmen cableway station. Flights from Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou and Chengdu are the usual connections, and the airport bus runs to the city for ¥10. Our airport transport guide covers the airports people connect through.

Staying in the city and commuting to the park is the single most expensive decision in a short trip: two hours a day in a shuttle, and the 07:00 slots gone by the time you arrive. If you are doing two mornings in the pillars, sleep in Wulingyuan.

Ask two questions when you book: how far the hotel is on foot from the gate you plan to use, and whether it can register a foreign passport.

Weather and closures

Mist is the whole risk, and it is seasonal.

November runs 5C to 16C in the valleys and several degrees colder on the ridges, with the first ice on the plank paths late in the month. Daylight is short: the light goes by 17:30, and the last shuttle buses leave the viewpoints around 17:00.

Mist is the thing that ruins trips, not cold. After rain the pillars disappear for hours and then reappear the following morning as a sea of cloud below the ridges, which is the photograph everyone wants and nobody can book.

Wind and ice close things. Cableways stop in high wind, the glass walkways close when the rock ices, and Tianmen suspends one of its three routes during maintenance. Check the operator's notice that morning rather than the guidebook.

Keep a spare morning. Two nights in Wulingyuan with one flexible one turns bad weather into a rearranged day.

December to February brings snow on the ridges and the fewest visitors, at the cost of closed paths and a shorter day. April and October are the two months to aim for if the dates are yours to choose; our city season guide has the monthly figures.

What goes wrong

Five mistakes, each costing an afternoon or a ticket.

1

Booking one ticket and expecting to see both parks.

The fix: Wulingyuan and Tianmen are separate purchases, separate bookings and 40 km apart. Budget for both or choose one.

2

Arriving without a timed slot.

The fix: slots are released about a week ahead and the 07:00 to 08:00 window is the one that matters. Booked-out mornings are normal from April to October.

3

Assuming the cable cars are in the ticket.

The fix: they are not. The elevator and the three cableways add ¥150 to ¥250 a head for a normal two-day visit, and they are bought at the station, not at the gate.

4

Sleeping in the city and commuting twice.

The fix: 80 to 90 minutes each way from the city to the gates. One night in Wulingyuan buys back a morning.

5

Treating Tianmen as a two-hour stop.

The fix: the cableway ride alone is 28 minutes each way, before the walkways, the cave and the escalators. Five hours is the honest figure.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions that decide the shape of the trip.

How many days do you need in Zhangjiajie?
Two full days for Wulingyuan alone, three to add Tianmen Mountain, four if you want the glass bridge or a second morning in the pillars without rushing.
Should I see Tianmen Mountain or Wulingyuan?
They are different trips. Wulingyuan is the sandstone pillars people call the Avatar mountains and needs two mornings. Tianmen is one mountain above the city with a long cableway, cliff walkways and the cave arch, and takes five to seven hours. If you have two days, take Wulingyuan.
Is the Wulingyuan ticket valid for four days?
Yes. The gate ticket is ¥165 and the version that includes the park shuttle buses is ¥236, both valid for four consecutive days. Insurance is ¥3 and optional, and you still need a timed entry slot for each morning.
Are the cable cars and the Bailong Elevator included in the ticket?
No. The Bailong Elevator is ¥65 one way, the Tianzi cable car ¥72, Yangjiajie ¥76, Huangshizhai ¥65 and the Ten Mile Gallery train ¥38. Budget ¥150 to ¥250 per person for the rides you actually use.
What is Zhangjiajie like in November?
Cold and often cloudy. Expect 5C to 16C in the valleys and several degrees less on the ridges, with mist that hides the pillars for hours and clears the morning after rain. Wind can close the cableways, so keep a spare morning.
Which station do I arrive at, and how do I reach Wulingyuan?
Zhangjiajie West is the high-speed station, about two hours from Changsha South and an hour from Fenghuang. The official shuttle from the station to Wulingyuan costs ¥13 and takes about 40 minutes; a taxi is ¥80 to ¥120.
Should I stay in Wulingyuan or in Zhangjiajie city?
Wulingyuan for the park, because the East gate is a short walk from most of the town's hotels. The city for Tianmen Mountain and for the train, since the cableway station is there. A three-day trip usually means two nights in Wulingyuan and one in the city.
Which way do I walk Golden Whip Stream?
Downhill, from the Forest Park gate at the south end towards Shuiraosimen, about 7.5 km and two and a half hours on the flat. Finish at the Bailong Elevator and ride up to Yuanjiajie rather than walking up.
