Where to Stay in Shanghai

The neighbourhoods, compared honestly. Which area suits your trip, what you will pay in each, and the details the booking sites do not tell you.

Shanghai is not one place. It is several cities sharing an address, and the one you sleep in sets the tone of the whole trip. Choose the area first; the hotel is the easy part.
• • •

What is the best area to stay in Shanghai?

For a first visit, stay in Jing'an or near People's Square. Jing'an is the most balanced base: quiet lanes, the city's best restaurant strip within walking distance, and Metro Lines 2, 7 and 14 at Jing'an Temple station (Line 12 is one stop away at Nanjing West Road). People's Square is the transport heart of the city. The French Concession rewards visitors who prefer cafés and tree-lined streets over crowds; the Bund and Lujiazui are for skyline views, at a premium. Avoid committing to Pudong unless your work is there.

Indicative rates for a standard double room: ¥600-1,500 in Jing'an, ¥800-1,800 in the Concession, ¥1,200-3,000 on the Bund.

Jing'an: the balanced base

Our default recommendation for a first visit.

Why it works

  • Location: central Puxi, on Metro Lines 2, 7 and 14 at Jing'an Temple station, with Line 12 at Nanjing West Road a short walk away. Line 2 runs direct to both airports and to the Bund.
  • Food: Julu Road, Fumin Road and Yanping Road form the best walkable restaurant strip in the city - ramen, tacos, Sichuan, Shanghainese, whatever you want tonight.
  • Atmosphere: quiet residential lanes a step away from Nanjing West Road's shopping canyon. You sleep well and you are never far from life.
  • Mix: international chains, boutique design hotels and serviced apartments sit side by side, so every price level is covered.
Watch for: the exact area called "Jing'an" on booking sites is large. South of Yan'an Road it bleeds into the Concession; north it gets more commercial. Check the hotel's street address, not just the district name.
• • •

People's Square: the transport heart

The most central base, at the best prices.

Why it works

  • Hub: Metro Lines 1, 2 and 8 all meet here. To either airport, to the Bund, to the old city - nothing is more than twenty minutes.
  • Landmarks: the Shanghai Museum, the Urban Planning Exhibition Centre and the start of pedestrian Nanjing Road are on the doorstep.
  • Value: this is where the city's 4-star stock concentrates at ¥500-1,200, a level hard to match elsewhere.
Be aware: the immediate station exits are crowded and touristy day and night. Pick a hotel a few streets off the main axis and you keep the convenience without the chaos.
• • •

The French Concession: the romantic choice

Plane trees, lane houses and slow mornings.

Why it works

  • Beauty: Wukang Road, Anfu Road and Fuxing Park are the most photogenic streets in China's largest city - and the quietest of the central areas.
  • Cafés: the best independent coffee and bakery scene in Shanghai, concentrated around Wukang and Yongkang roads.
  • Stay: restored shikumen townhouses and small boutique hotels that give the international chains on the other side of town a run for their money.
Trade-off: the Concession is not on a main Metro artery. You will walk to stations or take short Didi rides more than anywhere else, and weekend prices here climb first.
• • •

The Bund: the first-impression stay

The postcard view, at a postcard price.

Why it works

  • The view: waking up to the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu is the single most memorable hotel experience Shanghai has to offer.
  • The strip: the historic waterfront hotels - the Peace, the Fairmont, the Waldorf - are walking distance from the Bund promenade and the old city.
Be aware: you pay a heavy premium (¥1,200-3,000 and up), the classic hotels book out weeks ahead, and the immediate area is thin on everyday restaurants. It is a stay for one or two nights, not a base for a week.
• • •

Lujiazui: the business district

Across the river, up in the towers.

Why it works

  • Skyline stays: if your dream is to sleep above the clouds, this is the only side of the river that delivers it.
  • Corporate: the Ritz-Carlton, Grand Hyatt, Jumeirah and their neighbours exist for business travellers - and it shows in the service.
Trade-off: everything you came to see is on the other side of the river. Every single day trip, dinner and stroll starts with a tunnel crossing. Stay here only if your meetings are in Pudong.
• • •

Qiantan: the calm alternative

Shanghai's newest quarter, green and quiet.

Why it works

  • Space: wide boulevards, riverside parks and none of the street noise. A genuine change of pace if you have young children or a long stay.
  • New: the hotels here are recent, well priced (¥800-1,500) and close to the Oriental Sports Centre and the expo halls.
Trade-off: it is a twenty-minute Metro ride to the city's core. Fine for a retreat; wrong for a whirlwind first visit.

Booking practicalities

Check the property accepts foreign passports

Almost all licensed hotels register international guests without issue. Small guesthouses and short-stay apartments sometimes lack the licence to host foreign visitors - confirm in the booking notes or by message before you pay. At check-in you must show your passport; the hotel registers it with the police, which is routine and takes two minutes.

When prices move

  • Weekends: Friday and Saturday nights run 20-40% above weekday rates in Jing'an and the Concession.
  • Peak season: October-November (the best weather) and the Golden Week holidays (Labour Day, National Day, Chinese New Year) sell out far ahead. Book early or avoid those dates.
  • July-August: hot and humid, but prices dip - a good time to buy a better hotel for less.

Book smart

  • Compare Booking.com, Agoda, Trip.com and the hotel's own site; the house rate is sometimes lowest.
  • Luggage storage is standard - most hotels hold bags for free on check-out day. Use it and fly out on a last full day.
  • Pay with a card via Alipay where you can; it is smooth and you avoid the hotel's currency surcharge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Shanghai for tourists?

Jing'an is the best all-round base for tourists: quiet residential streets, strong Metro connections on Lines 2, 7, 12 and 14, and the city's best restaurant strip within walking distance. For a first visit, Jing'an or People's Square balances sightseeing, food and transport better than anywhere else.

Where should I stay in Shanghai for my first visit?

Base yourself in Jing'an or near People's Square. Both put you within easy Metro reach of the Bund, the old city and the French Concession, with good hotels at every price level. Choose Jing'an for the best food within walking distance, or People's Square for the most central transport hub.

Is it better to stay in Pudong or Puxi?

For most visitors, Puxi (the west side of the river) is better. The Bund, the old city, the French Concession and nearly all of the sightseeing are on this side. Stay in Pudong's Lujiazui only if your work is there or you want the skyline view from your window - you will pay more and cross the river for everything else.

Can foreigners stay anywhere in Shanghai?

Nearly all licensed hotels can register foreign guests. Small guesthouses and homestays sometimes lack the licence to host international visitors - check that the property accepts foreign passports before booking. You must have your passport with you for check-in; all hotels register it with the local police.
