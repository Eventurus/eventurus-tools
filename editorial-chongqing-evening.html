Chongqing, at Six in the Evening

A city with no reliable ground floor, and dinner across three kitchens.

By EVENTURUS / 20 October 2026 / 6 min read / Chongqing

Chongqing does not do flat. The city grew up a mountainside, and an address here tells you very little: buildings have entrances on different floors, and streets cross above rooftops. Locals call it the 8D city. Everyone else calls it the city where the map stops helping.

With more than thirty million residents, it is one of the largest cities in the world. The food came out of that working river port, shaped by dock workers passing through, and the two things it is known for are hotpot and noodles topped with chilli oil and stewed peas.

“The city where the map stops helping.”

The walk runs daily from six in the evening and takes three and a half hours, about two and a half kilometres on foot, with food at three local restaurants, a dessert and the night view at the end. Groups are two to ten.

We curate a short list of routes, and we check the details on each one before we sell it. This is one of them: one evening, three kitchens, and the walk between them.

Stop one

The building that lies about your floor

Twenty-two storeys above a street you thought was the ground.

Kuixing Building is one stop on the route. You stand on what looks like the ground floor, look over the edge, and find you are twenty-two storeys above another street. The itinerary calls it one of the best places to understand why the city reads as stacked rather than spread.

Stop two

One bowl, more than ten years

One dish, a decade and more.

A bowl of xiaomian from Mr Gong, who has spent more than a decade on this one dish: handmade noodles, slow-cooked yellow peas, minced pork, silky tofu pudding and chilli oil. It is the city's comfort food.

Chongqing people will argue about noodles the way other cities argue about football.

Stop three

What they ate before hotpot

The table before hotpot.

Long before the hotpot reputation arrived, locals gathered around tables filled with small shared dishes served with rice or tofu: steamed pork belly, braised beef, seasonal vegetables.

This is the part that explains the rest of the meal.

“This is the part that explains the rest of the meal.”

Stop four

Walking the mountain city

Stairs, lanes, and a wartime capital.

The route goes through the streets around Jiefangbei and Daijia Alley, where old stone staircases and hidden lanes sit beneath the modern skyline. This was a major river port and a wartime capital, and before roads and railways, tea, salt, people and ideas all moved through here. Merchants, journalists, labourers and families have passed through for generations.

You will climb. The city does not offer an alternative.

Stop five

Hotpot, the city's obsession

Beef tallow, chillies, peppercorns.

The savoury end of the evening is a local neighbourhood hotpot restaurant. Chongqing hotpot started along the Yangtze more than a hundred years ago as a simple meal for dock workers and boat crews. It is now a city obsession.

Beef tallow, fiery chillies, numbing Sichuan peppercorns. The numbness is not a flaw in the cooking.

Stop six

Hongyadong, and something sweet

The view, and then dessert.

The route finishes at Hongyadong, where traditional architecture meets the modern skyline, and then a handmade dessert a few steps away, overlooking Qiansimen Bridge.

By then it is half past nine.

What it is, and who it suits

This is a dinner, not a cooking class. You eat at three restaurants, walk between them, and finish with a view.

It suits anyone who wants to understand a city through what people eat. It does not suit anyone who cannot manage stairs.
