E-Bike Rental in China

What a foreign visitor can legally ride, what the counter will ask for, and where the 25 km/h line falls.

The short version

  • What is legal. An electric bicycle, 电动自行车, is a non-motor vehicle: design speed up to 25 km/h, rated motor up to 400 W, nominal battery up to 48 V, a local plate, no driving licence. The rider must be 16 or older.
  • The line. Above 25 km/h it is a motorcycle. A moped of up to 50 km/h needs an F, E or D licence and a blue plate; a motorcycle above 50 km/h needs an E or D licence and a yellow plate. Both need compulsory motor insurance.
  • Licences. An overseas licence cannot be used in China. A temporary driving permit, for temporary entrants, lasts three months for a short stay, and up to a year once the stay or residence passes three months. It can cover a motorcycle, including a rented Chinese one.
  • Cost. Yangshuo 20 to 40 yuan a day, Dali 40 to 80, Sanya 30 to 60. Shanghai is the exception: MUTE Garage, the operator this page has checked, rents by the week or month rather than the day, from 200 yuan a week or 330 yuan a month.
  • Enforcement. 15 km/h is the ceiling in a bike lane, and Shanghai states the limit as 15 km/h without qualifying the lane. Helmets are required in Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Hebei, Beijing, Shenzhen and Shandong. In Shanghai and Beijing an adult passenger and a phone in hand are both out.
Verified 19 October 2026. Every legal figure and every price below is traced to the notes at the foot of this page. No phone calls were made: the legal material is published regulation and reporting of it, and the prices are what operators and travel reports published in 2026.

Why the pedals no longer tell you anything

The old advice was simple: pedals mean e-bike, no pedals mean motorcycle. That advice died on 1 September 2025, when GB 17761-2024 dropped the requirement to fit pedals to every model. A vehicle without pedals can now be a legal electric bicycle.

Ask the shop two questions instead: what is the top speed, and where is the plate. A compliant electric bicycle is designed to a maximum of 25 km/h and carries a non-motor vehicle plate. If the answer to the first question is "much faster" and the second is a shrug, the machine is very likely a motorcycle, and if it carries no plate it should not be on the road at all.

Registration is not automatic. A published FAQ from Dongguan states that a two-wheeled vehicle without pedals is not an electric bicycle and will not be registered there, although GB 17761-2024 no longer requires pedals on every model. If you are buying rather than renting, check the local office before you pay.

What the counter will ask for

For an electric bicycle

  • Passport. One English-language operator in Yangshuo asks for a photograph of the passport page. Carry it whatever the shop.
  • Deposit. 100 to 500 yuan in the tourist towns priced below. The same Yangshuo operator takes 50 per cent of the bike's value in cash; Sanya shops set their own terms.
  • Documents as security. A 2026 travel report from Dali describes shops holding an ID card instead of a deposit and advises paying cash rather than leaving a document.
  • Age. The legal minimum is 16.

Before you ride off

  • Photograph the bike, including scratches, the plate and the battery gauge.
  • Test both brakes and the throttle cut-off. A bike that lurches when you push it has a modified controller.
  • Check the plate is attached and legible. A plate in the shop's drawer is not a plate.
  • Agree the return time and the late fee in writing, even if it is a WeChat message.
  • Ask what the rental covers if you hit someone or come off.

Shared e-bikes, and where they are restricted

A 2017 ten-ministry policy on internet rental bicycles did not encourage shared e-bikes. A 2023 reply from Guangzhou's Panyu district named Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen as cities that had banned or discouraged the business, and said Guangzhou did not encourage it either. That is not the same as a simple ban: Beijing's 2026 regulation issues special plates to e-bikes used for internet rental.

Numbers are set city by city, and the sources are uneven. Nanning's municipal government says the city has five internet-rental bicycle companies and has summoned them for talks as part of a campaign against shared e-bikes. The rest comes from industry research rather than government notices: that research puts Changsha at eight licensed brands, with Hello, Meituan and Qingju at more than 100,000 vehicles each, records Kunming's 2026 review selecting three operators for the main urban area, and names Guiyang, Zunyi, Liupanshui and other places as having operations.

Whether a passport works in a given operator's app is worth testing before you rely on it, so keep a shop rental as the fallback.

The licence question, settled

"An overseas licence cannot be used in China, and a temporary driving permit must be obtained instead," the Shanghai public security bureau replied on 23 June 2025. An International Driving Permit does not change that.

The permit is designed for temporary entrants. It is issued to you, not to a vehicle, and under circular 公交管〔2019〕465号 it lets you drive a rented Chinese motorcycle as well as one you bring into the country. A short stay gets three months, and a stay or residence of more than three months gets up to a year. The circular asks for your travel document and your overseas licence. Shanghai's public security bureau asks for the travel document in full, which means the passport with a valid visa or residence permit, plus a Chinese translation of the licence, the application form and two one-inch photographs.

What the permit does not solve is supply. Before you pay for one, ask the shop whether the motorcycle you would ride is registered and carries the compulsory insurance that a motorcycle needs to be on the road at all. A permitted rider on an unregistered bike is still on an illegal bike.

The one question worth asking. "Is this bike plated, and is its design speed 25?" A shop that answers both without hesitating is a shop that expects to be checked.

Helmets, insurance and the morning after

China requires compulsory motor insurance for motorcycles. An electric bicycle is not a motor vehicle, so that requirement does not reach it, and Shanghai's regulation goes no further than encouraging third-party, personal accident and property cover. Ask what the rental includes and get the answer in a message rather than a nod.

If you hit a pedestrian, the claim sits between you, the other party and the police. Ride slowly, and treat 15 km/h as the number that matters rather than a technicality.

After a crash, three things count: the police report, the plate number and the rental contract. Call the shop before you move the bike, photograph everything, and do not agree a cash settlement at the scene without a police record.

Check your travel policy before you ride. A scooter above 25 km/h is a motor vehicle, and an International Driving Permit is not a Chinese licence. Whether your policy covers you on one without a Chinese permit is worth confirming in writing before you pay.

Charging and range

Ask what the battery actually does rather than what the sticker claims. A loop of 127 to 130 km such as Erhai needs a long-range bike or a charge stop. In Dali, charging has been quoted at about 10 yuan a time.

Charging in your room is the habit worth breaking before you arrive. Shanghai bans charging in the shared parts of a building, in escape routes and in the indoor areas of crowded places, and running a cable from a socket attracts a 50 to 200 yuan fine. Beijing bans taking an e-bike battery into a residential building from 1 May 2026. A battery fire is not a risk worth taking whatever the rule says about the room. Charge at the shop or at a public point.

If the police stop you

If you are stopped, carry your passport and the rental contract or a photograph of it. The plate identifies the vehicle, which usually settles the question of who it belongs to without a long conversation.

The penalties in the table above come from the sources at the foot of this page: local regulations for most rows, national law for the drink row, and a reported camera enforcement figure for speed. Several leave a warning or a fine to the officer's discretion. Ask for a receipt whatever is decided. If the bike turns out to be unplated in Shanghai, the 50 to 200 yuan fine lands on the rider, so check the plate at the counter and ask the shop to swap the bike before you set off.

Buying instead of renting

For a stay of months, price a purchase against a monthly rental, then look hard at registration. Guangzhou requires a foreign owner to produce a passport, a Chinese translation of it, the accommodation registration certificate issued by the police, and a visa or residence permit valid for three months or more, along with the purchase invoice, the conformity certificate and the vehicle itself. Other cities set their own lists. In Guangzhou that three-month visa or permit is the binding constraint.

The bike also has to qualify. From 1 December 2025 every electric bicycle sold in China has to meet GB 17761-2024. Separately, any electric bicycle sold new has to hold certification and carry the CCC mark on the body. Guangzhou's list requires the purchase invoice and the conformity certificate, which rules out a bike sold without papers. As above, city practice can differ from the national standard, so check the office before paying.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners rent an e-bike in China?
Yes. An electric bicycle, 电动自行车, is legally a non-motor vehicle, so no driving licence is required. The rider must be at least 16. Carry your passport, and confirm that the bike carries a local non-motor vehicle plate before you pay.
Do I need a licence to ride an e-bike in China?
Not for an electric bicycle, which is capped at a design speed of 25 km/h. Above that line the vehicle is a motorcycle: an electric moped of up to 50 km/h needs an F, E or D licence and a blue plate, and an electric motorcycle above 50 km/h needs an E or D licence and a yellow plate. Both categories also carry compulsory motor insurance and cannot be ridden on a foreign licence.
Can I use an international driving permit in China?
No. An International Driving Permit does not override the rule: the Shanghai public security bureau states that an overseas licence cannot be used in China. A temporary entrant can apply for a temporary driving permit instead, valid three months where the stay is short and up to a year where the stay or residence passes three months. Motorcycles are covered, including a rented Chinese one. Ask the shop whether the bike you intend to ride is registered and insured before you pay for the permit.
How much does it cost to rent an e-bike in China?
Travel reports from 2026 put Yangshuo at 20 to 40 yuan a day, with West Street shops quoting up to 150, and Dali at 40 to 80 yuan a day, or 130 to 180 for a bike that can circle the lake. Sanya runs 30 to 60 yuan a day. In Shanghai, MUTE Garage, the operator featured below, rents by the week or month rather than the day: from 200 yuan a week or 330 yuan a month, with a helmet, raincoat and damage cover included.
Do I have to wear a helmet on an e-bike in China?
There is no national penalty for riding without one, so it depends on the province or city. Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Hebei require one, Beijing has since 1 May 2026, and Shenzhen and Shandong fine it. The penalties run from a warning or 20 to 50 yuan in Shanghai, Jiangsu and Zhejiang, with Jiangsu at 20 for a first offence and 50 for a second; a warning or 10 to 50 in Beijing; a warning or 10 to 50 in Hebei where an order to correct goes unheeded; 200 in Shenzhen, on the rider; and 20 in Shandong where a repeat offender refuses to correct it. Ask for a helmet with the bike and refuse one without a certification mark.
Can I carry a passenger on an e-bike in China?
No adults in Shanghai or Beijing. Shanghai allows one child aged 12 or younger, in a fixed seat if the child is 6 or younger. Beijing allows an adult driver to carry one child aged 16 or younger in the fixed seat behind the rider from 1 May 2026. Elsewhere the rule is set locally.
Are there shared e-bikes in China?
In some cities, but the big four restrict them. A 2017 ten-ministry policy did not encourage shared e-bikes, and a 2023 reply from Guangzhou's Panyu district named Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen as cities that had banned or discouraged the business, with Guangzhou not encouraging it either. That is not a simple ban: Beijing's 2026 regulation issues special plates to e-bikes used for internet rental. Nanning's municipal government says it has five internet-rental bicycle companies and has summoned them for talks as part of a campaign against shared e-bikes. Industry research, rather than government notices, puts Changsha at eight licensed brands, records Kunming's 2026 review selecting three operators for the main urban area, and names Guiyang, Zunyi, Liupanshui and other places as having operations.
Can I charge the battery in my hotel room?
Beijing bans taking an e-bike battery into a residential building from 1 May 2026, and Shanghai bans charging in the shared parts of a building, in escape routes and in the indoor areas of crowded places, with a 50 to 200 yuan fine for running a cable. A battery fire is not a risk worth taking whatever the rule says about the room. Charge at the rental shop or at a public charging point.
Should I buy an e-bike instead of renting one?
Only for a long stay. Guangzhou requires a foreign owner to produce a passport, a Chinese translation of it, the accommodation registration certificate issued by the police, and a visa or residence permit valid for three months or more, along with the purchase invoice, the conformity certificate and the vehicle itself. Other cities set their own lists, and in Guangzhou that three-month visa or permit is the binding constraint.
