Where to Stay in Beijing

The neighbourhoods, compared honestly. Which area suits your trip, what you will pay in each, and the details the booking sites do not tell you.

Beijing is a city that humbles the planner. Distances are imperial, traffic is a force of nature, and the right hotel can save you an hour of every day. Choose the area first; the hotel is the easy part.
• • •

What is the best area to stay in Beijing?

For a first visit, stay in Wangfujing or Qianmen. Both keep the Forbidden City and Tiananmen within walking distance, sit directly on the Metro (Lines 1 and 2), and cover every price level. The hutongs around Gulou suit visitors who want courtyard hotels and charm over convenience. Sanlitun is the modern choice for food and nightlife. Avoid committing to the far suburbs - Beijing's sights are spread out, so central plus Metro beats everything.

Indicative rates for a standard double room: ¥600-1,500 in Wangfujing, ¥500-1,200 in Qianmen, ¥700-1,500 in the hutongs.

Wangfujing: the first-timer base

Our default recommendation for a first visit.

Why it works

  • Location: a fifteen-minute walk from the Forbidden City's Meridian Gate, on Metro Line 1 with the Airport Express connection at Dongzhimen a short ride away.
  • Hotels: the densest concentration of international chains in the city centre, from business hotels to the grand classics.
  • Life: the Wangfujing pedestrian street is touristy, but the surrounding hutong lanes and the snack street behind it are the real thing.
Watch for: the exact zone on booking sites is wide. North of the palace the area gets quieter; south it runs into the Dongcheng residential lanes. Check the street address, not just the district name.
• • •

Qianmen: the old-town choice

History on the doorstep, at the best prices in the centre.

Why it works

  • Atmosphere: the gates, the arrow towers and the restored commercial streets of Dashilan put you in the old city the moment you step out.
  • Position: Tiananmen Square is a fifteen-minute walk north; Metro Line 2 and Line 8 run through the area.
  • Value: some of the best 4-star stock in central Beijing sits here at ¥500-1,200, hard to match anywhere else near the centre.
Be aware: the immediate square is busy from early morning (security checks nearby), and some lanes are tourist-herded during the day. Pick a hotel a street or two off the main axis.
• • •

Gulou & Shichahai: the hutong stay

Courtyard hotels and the quietest version of the old city.

Why it works

  • Charm: restored siheyuan courtyard hotels, grey-tile lanes, and the lake at Shichahai - the most characterful sleep in Beijing.
  • Night life: the Drum and Bell Tower district is the best bar and café quarter of the old city, without Sanlitun's noise.
  • Position: Metro Line 8 runs north-south through the district and reaches the Summer Palace line with one change.
Trade-off: courtyard hotels are boutique in scale and price, and the lanes can be a maze after dark. Worth it for the character; less ideal for heavy-luggage airport days.
• • •

Sanlitun & the CBD: the modern choice

Skyscrapers, the city's best food and its loudest bars.

Why it works

  • Food and drink: the Sanlitun area has the densest concentration of international and upmarket Chinese restaurants in the city - a serious argument if dinner matters.
  • Business: the Guomao CBD east of here holds the Ritz-Carlton, the China World and the Rosewood - the corporate hotels, built for work and a place to read on a Sunday.
  • Modern Beijing: this is the city's present tense - glass towers, designer malls, and the embassy quarter nearby.
Trade-off: it is farther from the old city - twenty minutes by Metro to Wangfujing, longer to the hutongs. For a short first trip, weigh the restaurant scene against the sightseeing distance.
• • •

Zhongguancun: the quiet northwest

China's technology district, and the gateway to the Summer Palace.

Why it works

  • Position for the west: the Summer Palace and Old Summer Palace are close - a genuine convenience if they are on your list.
  • Value and calm: better rates than the centre and a fraction of the tourist traffic, on Metro Line 4.
  • Corporate: a natural base for anyone visiting Beijing's tech companies or the universities.
Trade-off: the palace district and the Great Wall trips all leave from the centre, so you will ride the Metro every day. Fine for a second visit; a stretch for a first.

Booking practicalities

Check the property accepts foreign passports

Almost all licensed hotels register international guests without issue. Small guesthouses and short-stay apartments sometimes lack the licence to host foreign visitors - confirm in the booking notes before you pay. At check-in you must show your passport; the hotel registers it with the police, which is routine and takes two minutes.

When prices move

  • Peak weeks: National Day (1-7 October), Labour Day and Chinese New Year sell out weeks ahead across the city centre. Avoid those dates or book very early.
  • Autumn (October-November): the best weather and the highest prices; weekends in Wangfujing and the hutongs run 20-40% above weekday rates.
  • Winter (December-February): cold but uncrowded, and rates drop sharply - the value window for a city break.

Book smart

  • Compare Booking.com, Agoda, Trip.com and the hotel's own site; the house rate is sometimes lowest.
  • Airport access matters in Beijing: from Wangfujing allow about an hour to Capital by Airport Express plus Metro, and more to Daxing. Factor it into your last-day planning.
  • Luggage storage is standard - most hotels hold bags free on check-out day. Use it for a final full day.
