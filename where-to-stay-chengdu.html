Where to Stay in Chengdu

Chengdu rewards the slow traveller, which makes the area you book more important than the sights you list. Here is what each neighbourhood is actually like, what it costs, and which airport your booking lands you at.

The short version

  • First visit: stay between Chunxi Road and Tianfu Square, where Metro Lines 2 and 3 meet and the best food is on foot.
  • Courtyard hotels: Kuanzhai Alley, for the restored lanes and the courtyard conversions inside them.
  • Pandas: Wuhou, with Metro Line 3 to Panda Avenue and a shuttle for the last stretch to the reserve.
  • Budget: Eastern Suburb Memory, the industrial district east of the centre, where rates sit below the rest.
  • Airports: most international flights land at Tianfu (TFU) and domestic at Shuangliu (CTU); Metro Line 18 serves Tianfu and Line 10 serves Shuangliu.
Chengdu does not reward the checklist. The pleasure of the city is a long lunch and a slow afternoon, so choose the neighbourhood you want to wake up in rather than the one closest to a list of monuments.
• • •

What is the best area to stay in Chengdu?

For a first visit, stay between Chunxi Road and Tianfu Square. That band puts two metro lines within a few minutes' walk, keeps the best food on foot, and covers every price level. Kuanzhai Alley suits visitors who want courtyard hotels and old-city atmosphere. Wuhou is the better base if the panda reserve and the temples are your priority: Line 3 runs to Panda Avenue without a change, and a shuttle covers the last stretch to the reserve.

Indicative rates for a standard double room: ¥700-1,600 around Chunxi Road, ¥500-1,100 near Tianfu Square, ¥800-1,800 in the courtyard hotels off Kuanzhai Alley.

Chunxi Road & Taikoo Li: the first-timer base

Our default recommendation for a first visit.

Why it works

  • Location: the junction of Metro Lines 2 and 3 at Chunxi Road station, which reaches most of the city with one change.
  • Food: the density of restaurants here is the reason to stay here. The lanes behind Taikoo Li hold the city's best casual eating, from hotpot to cold noodles.
  • Hotels: the widest spread in the city, from business chains to the luxury towers on the edge of Taikoo Li.
Watch for: the district label on booking sites is applied generously. A hotel billed as Chunxi Road can sit a twenty-minute walk east. Check the street address and the nearest metro exit, not the district name.
• • •

Tianfu Square & People's Park: the older city

The calmest central base, and the cheapest of the three.

Why it works

  • Position: Tianfu Square is the crossing of Lines 1 and 2, so every direction is a single change. People's Park is one stop west on Line 2.
  • Atmosphere: the teahouse in People's Park is the last place in central Chengdu where an afternoon costs almost nothing. This is the old city at walking pace.
  • Value: hotels here cost less than Chunxi Road for the same standard, because the address carries less prestige.
Trade-off: the square itself is a traffic interchange rather than a neighbourhood. Stay on the People's Park side, or a block off the square, and the area becomes pleasant.
• • •

Kuanzhai Alley: the courtyard stay

The most characterful sleep in the city, at a price.

Why it works

  • Charm: restored Qing lanes and converted courtyard houses. The best properties here are small, and the rooms open onto a shared court rather than a corridor.
  • Position: Metro Line 4 stops at Kuanzhai Alley, and People's Park is a ten-minute walk south-east through the lanes.
  • Evenings: the alley empties after seven, which is when it becomes worth staying in rather than visiting.
Be aware: the alley is a tourist site by day and the crowds start early. Courtyard rooms are also smaller than the rate suggests, and not every property has a lift or a car-accessible entrance. Ask before booking if you have heavy luggage.
• • •

Wuhou & Jinli: the sightseeing base

Best value in the centre, with a through metro ride to the pandas.

Why it works

  • Pandas: Line 3 runs to Panda Avenue, then a shuttle to the reserve. Chunxi Road sits on the same line, so either base gives you a direct ride.
  • Sights: Wuhou Shrine and the Jinli street-food lane are adjacent, and the Tibetan quarter around Wuhou Temple Road is the most distinctive walk in the city.
  • Value: the same four-star standard as the centre at a lower rate, because it sits two metro stops south.
Watch for: Jinli is a reconstruction built for visitors, and the food there is priced accordingly. The lanes behind it, towards Wuhou Temple Road, are where people actually eat.
• • •

Eastern Suburb Memory: the creative district

For a second visit, or anyone who has seen the old city.

Why it works

  • Character: a factory complex turned into galleries, small venues and bars. Chengdu's design and music scene is here rather than in the centre.
  • Position: Metro Line 8 stops at the district, which puts the centre twenty minutes away and the east side on foot.
  • Value: the cheapest rates of any area on this page, in converted lofts and new-built hotels.
Trade-off: this is not a first-visit base. The classic sights are all a metro ride away, and the district is quiet on weekday mornings. Choose it for the evenings, not the itinerary.

Booking practicalities

Which airport you land at

Chengdu has two airports on opposite sides of the city, so check your booking before you plan the transfer. Tianfu (TFU) is the new international hub, 51 km south-east of the centre. Shuangliu (CTU) is the older airport, 16 km south-west, and still takes most domestic and regional flights.

  • From TFU: Metro Line 18 to Chengdu South Railway Station, ¥10, about 37 minutes on an express departure; change to Line 1 for Tianfu Square, roughly 75 minutes in total. Trains run 06:00-23:30.
  • From TFU by bus: dedicated airport buses run around the clock on separate routes, one to Chunxi Road and one to Chengdu East Railway Station, 50-100 minutes depending on the line, ¥15 by day and ¥25 overnight.
  • From TFU by taxi: ¥150-200 plus the expressway toll, 60-80 minutes depending on traffic.
  • From CTU: Metro Line 10 into the city, about 30-35 minutes, ¥2-8 by distance. A taxi to the centre is ¥50-90.
  • Between the two: Line 19 links TFU and CTU directly in about 50 minutes for ¥10.

Check the property accepts foreign passports

Almost all licensed hotels register international guests without issue, and no separate permit is required in order to take foreign guests. A minority of small guesthouses, courtyard conversions and short-stay apartments still turn foreign visitors away - common in the boutique stock around Kuanzhai Alley - which is not permitted. Confirm in the booking notes before you pay. At check-in you show your passport and the hotel registers it with the police, which is routine.

When prices move

  • Peak weeks: National Day (1-7 October), Labour Day and Chinese New Year sell out weeks ahead, and the city fills with domestic travellers.
  • Spring (March-April) and autumn (September-October): the best weather and the highest rates; weekends are priced above weekdays.
  • Summer (July-August): hot and humid, and the low season for a reason. Rates fall, and so does the appeal of walking anywhere at two in the afternoon.

Book smart

  • Compare Booking.com, Agoda, Trip.com and the hotel's own site; booking direct sometimes beats the platforms.
  • Allow for the airport on your last day. From Chunxi Road, TFU is a 75-minute metro run or an hour by taxi plus toll, so a morning flight means leaving before six.
  • Ask reception to hold your bags after check-out, which buys you a final afternoon. Confirm at booking whether there is a charge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Chengdu for tourists?

Chunxi Road and Tianfu Square are the best bases for a first visit: both sit on two metro lines, keep the city's best food within walking distance, and cover every price level. Kuanzhai Alley suits visitors who want courtyard hotels. Wuhou is the quieter, cheaper base south of the centre, and Line 3 reaches Panda Avenue from there without a change, with a shuttle for the last stretch.

Which airport should I fly into, Tianfu or Shuangliu?

It is decided by your airline, not by you. Tianfu (TFU) handles most long-haul and international routes and sits 51 km south-east of the centre. Shuangliu (CTU) is 16 km south-west and takes mostly domestic and regional flights. Line 19 links the two directly, but allow an hour door to door, so confirm the airport code before you book a transfer.

How do I get from Tianfu airport to the city?

Metro Line 18 runs from the terminal to Chengdu South Railway Station in about 37 minutes on an express train, for 10 yuan. Change to Line 1 for Tianfu Square, which takes the total to roughly 75 minutes. The airport bus runs around the clock to Chunxi Road for 15 yuan by day and 25 yuan overnight. A taxi is 150-200 yuan plus the expressway toll.

Is it better to stay near Kuanzhai Alley or Chunxi Road?

Kuanzhai Alley for atmosphere and courtyard hotels, Chunxi Road for food, transport and price range. Kuanzhai Alley is a ten-minute walk from People's Park and one stop from the centre by metro, so it is not remote. If this is your first visit and you want to eat well without planning, choose Chunxi Road.

Can foreigners stay anywhere in Chengdu?

Nearly all licensed hotels register international guests without issue. Small guesthouses and courtyard conversions sometimes turn foreign visitors away, which is common in the boutique stock around Kuanzhai Alley, though the practice is not permitted. Confirm the property accepts foreign passports before booking.
