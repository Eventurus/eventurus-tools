Where to Stay in Wuzhen

Four bases, one decision: inside the gates or outside them. The answer changes what the town looks like at seven in the morning.

Wuzhen has two lives. Between nine and five it is a queue. Before and after, it is a canal town with laundry hanging over the water. Only one of those is available to day visitors.

At a glance

  • No railway station: arrive via Tongxiang, then 28 to 40 minutes by taxi.
  • Four bases, from ¥200 in Tongxiang to ¥3,000 inside Xizha.
  • Sleeping inside the gates is the only way to see the town before nine.
  • A single-entry ticket will not let you back in after lunch.
  • Shanghai Hongqiao to Tongxiang: 33 to 50 minutes, ¥40 to ¥62.
  • One night is enough; two only for dawn and dusk photography.

What is the best area to stay in Wuzhen?

Sleep inside Xizha if the town is the point: guests get the lanes before nine and after five, which day visitors never see. Outside the west gate is cheaper and a ten-minute walk from the entrance. Tongxiang is for late arrivals only, and Dongzha suits anyone who wants the quieter half.

The four bases

  • The experience: inside Xizha, the west scenic area, where official guesthouses and hotels sit on the canals and guests keep access after the gates close.
  • Quieter and cheaper: inside Dongzha, the east scenic area, less restored and more lived-in, with lower rates and fewer tour groups.
  • Value, still close: outside the west gate, a strip of ordinary hotels and guesthouses within a ten-minute walk of the entrance.
  • Late arrivals and budgets: Tongxiang, the county town 13 kilometres south, whose rail station sits 25 kilometres from the gate. Chain hotels and ordinary prices.
Rule of thumb: one night inside the gates is worth two nights outside. If the budget only stretches to one, spend it here.

Inside Xizha: the reason to come

Sleeping inside the west scenic area is the only way to see the town before nine and after five, when it belongs to residents.

What you are paying for

  • Access: guests of official properties can register for re-entry and walk the lanes after the day tickets expire, which is when the lanterns come on.
  • Mornings: the hour before the first coaches is the photograph everyone wants and almost nobody gets. It requires sleeping here.
  • Properties: a mix of restored merchant houses, courtyard inns and one or two larger hotels. Most are managed by the resort itself rather than by individual owners, and some also list on the booking apps.
Be aware: rooms are small, beds are firm and the bathrooms are old. You are paying for the address, not the thread count.
• • •

Inside Dongzha: the quieter half

Less polished, less crowded, and closer to how the town actually worked before tourism.

The quieter half

  • Character: workshops, soy sauce yards and residents going about their day, with fewer reconstructed facades than the west side.
  • Price: roughly half of Xizha rates for a comparable room, which makes a two-night stay affordable.
  • Evening: much dimmer and much quieter. If you want nightlife, this is the wrong side.
Watch for: the free shuttle between the two gates stops in the late afternoon. Miss it and you are walking thirty minutes or paying for a taxi.
• • •

Outside the west gate: value without the commute

Ordinary hotels within a ten-minute walk, which is close enough to be useful and far enough to be cheap.

Value without the commute

  • Price: a third of inside rates, with larger rooms and parking, which matters if you are driving. For gardens rather than canals, Suzhou is 90 minutes by road.
  • Access: you can walk to the gate in ten minutes and be inside before the first tour buses, though you will queue with everyone else rather than stroll past them.
  • Food: local restaurants outside the gate are cheaper and, in our experience, better than the set menus inside.
• • •

Tongxiang: the late-arrival option

A working city rather than a water town, useful only if you arrive after dark or refuse to pay scenic-area prices.

A bed, not an experience

  • Transport: the high-speed station is here, and taxis are available at any hour, unlike at the scenic area gates.
  • Price: chain hotels at business rates, with breakfast and reliable hot water.
  • When to use it: a late train, a missed bus, or a holiday weekend when everything inside is fully booked.
Be aware: you will commute 30 to 40 minutes each way. Two days of that costs more than the room saves. If you are still choosing a base, the delta guide compares all six.

How do you get to Wuzhen?

Wuzhen has no railway station. The route is Shanghai Hongqiao to Tongxiang in 33 to 50 minutes for ¥40 to ¥62, then 28 to 40 minutes by taxi for ¥50 to ¥100, or an hour by bus for ¥7. Door to door, allow two hours from central Shanghai.

Tickets, re-entry and boats

The rules that decide whether your stay works. A single-entry ticket is the most common mistake.

What the ticket covers

  • Two gates: the west and east scenic areas are ticketed separately, with a combined ticket that costs less than both together.
  • Single entry: standard tickets usually admit you once. Leave for lunch and you may need to pay again, unless you are staying at an official property inside and have registered for re-entry.
  • Passport: tickets are linked to identity and the gate scans your passport. A photograph on your phone will not work.

Boats and carts

  • Shared boat: the classic circuit, priced per person, departing when full.
  • Private boat: a flat rate for up to eight people, worth it at dusk for the light rather than the distance.
  • Electric carts: free for guests of official hotels inside, charged for everyone else, and the sensible way to move luggage.
Practical: the town runs almost entirely cashless. Link a card to Alipay or WeChat Pay before you arrive, because the bus driver cannot break a hundred.

When should you go?

Spring and autumn for weather, weekdays for the town itself. A festival night is spectacular and extremely busy, and the lanes are narrow enough that a crowd changes the experience entirely. If you have the choice, take a Tuesday in April over a Saturday in October.

By season

  • March to May: mist on the canals, mild air and good light at the edges of the day. Weekends are busy but manageable.
  • June to August: hot, humid and bright. Early mornings and evenings are the only comfortable hours, which is another argument for sleeping inside.
  • September to November: dry, clear and the most reliable window. Book two to three weeks ahead for a weekend.
  • December to February: cold and often damp, but the lanes are empty and rates fall. Some restaurants close between the New Year and spring.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions that come up before booking.

Is it worth staying overnight in Wuzhen?

Yes. Day visitors see the town between nine and five, when it is busiest. Guests at official properties inside can register for re-entry, so they get the lanes before nine and the lanterns after five, which amounts to a different town.

How do I get to Wuzhen from Shanghai?

High-speed train from Shanghai Hongqiao to Tongxiang in 33 to 50 minutes for ¥40 to ¥62, then a 28 to 40 minute taxi for ¥50 to ¥100. The local bus costs ¥7 in total but takes about an hour with a transfer at Wuzhen bus station.

Can I leave the scenic area and come back?

Standard tickets are usually single entry, so leaving for lunch may mean paying again. Guests at official properties inside can register for re-entry at the gate. Confirm the rule when you book, because it changes how you plan meals.

How much are the tickets?

The west and east scenic areas are ticketed separately, with a combined ticket that costs less than buying both. Expect around ¥150 for the west side and ¥110 for the east in high season, with prices moving by date and by channel.

Do I need cash in Wuzhen?

No. The town runs almost entirely cashless, including buses, boats and small shops, and drivers cannot break large notes. Link a foreign card to Alipay or WeChat Pay before you arrive rather than at a ticket window.
